Project Overview

Abstract

The rapid proliferation of low-cost commercial unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) has created new security threats from unauthorised drone operations. Conventional counter-UAS sensors – radar, optical/infrared cameras and acoustic arrays – are limited by line-of-sight requirements, weather susceptibility and cost. Passive radio-frequency (RF) sensing offers a resilient alternative, because nearly every commercial drone continuously exchanges control and video telemetry with its operator.

VARDAN is a research project that builds the RF sensing core of a multi-modal, edge-deployable counter-UAS system. The work faithfully reproduces published deep-learning baselines on the public DroneRF dataset, re-evaluates them under a strict recording-level protocol with zero session leakage, and proposes VARDHAN-v3, a lightweight (119,806-parameter) dual-domain neural network that jointly processes the raw time-domain waveform and a three-channel Fourier representation of each 2048-sample RF segment.

The central finding is a large generalisation gap: models that achieve 65–85% accuracy under the standard segment-level 10-fold cross-validation fall to 24.8–28.8% when tested on recording sessions never seen during training. The project documents this gap, its causes, and the evidence still required before edge deployment.

Problem Statement

Published RF-based drone classifiers on DroneRF report high accuracy, but they are evaluated with segment-level random splits. Because consecutive segments cut from the same recording share transmitter, receiver, channel and environment characteristics, a random split places near-identical segments in both the training and test sets. This segment-level leakage inflates reported accuracy and hides the true cross-recording generalisation ability that a deployed sensor would require.

Research Goals

  1. Reproduce baseline RF classification models faithfully from the literature and their released code.
  2. Establish standardised benchmarks, including a strict recording-level protocol with zero session overlap.
  3. Propose VARDHAN-v3, a lightweight dual-domain RF architecture for robust drone detection and identification.
  4. Develop an acoustic module for near-field detection.
  5. Integrate a computer-vision module and fuse all modalities adaptively.
  6. Optimise and deploy the system on ARM-class embedded and microcontroller platforms.
